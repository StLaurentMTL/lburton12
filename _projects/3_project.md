This project asks whether chronic loneliness can be predicted from structural characteristics that 
public health agencies already observe, such as income, housing stability, physical health burden, 
and access to transportation. Using the 2024 Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System (BRFSS), 
a nationally representative telephone survey of U.S. adults, we train and evaluate various 
machine learning models to identify respondents who report persistent loneliness. 
Identifying which structural conditions are most strongly associated with chronic loneliness has 
direct utility for public health policy: it allows programs with finite resources to prioritize 
the communities and interventions most likely to reach those at greatest risk.
